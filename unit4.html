01 / Multi-Cloud & Federation (Asked in 2024–25 & 2025–26)

Inter-Cloud Resource Management & OCC

Simple Definition

Inter-Cloud Resource Management is the coordinated sharing, scheduling, and migration of compute, storage, and network resources across multiple independent cloud providers (AWS, Azure, GCP, and private data centers) so they operate seamlessly as a unified global "cloud of clouds."

Two Core Inter-Cloud Topologies

  • Federated Cloud: Cloud providers formally collaborate behind the scenes to share idle capacity with each other during traffic peaks (transparent to the end customer).
  • Multi-Cloud: The customer directly deploys and manages workloads across two or more independent cloud providers without requiring provider-to-provider agreements.

Five Reasons Inter-Cloud Matters (Write in 7-Mark Answers)

  1. Eliminates Vendor Lock-In: Freedom to move apps across providers if one raises prices or changes terms.
  2. Infinite Bursting & Load Balancing: Small or private clouds borrow extra VMs from partner clouds during sudden spikes.
  3. High Availability & Disaster Recovery: Survives even a full single-provider regional outage by failing over to another cloud.
  4. Geo-Proximity & Low Latency: Runs services in whichever partner data center is physically closest to end users.
  5. Cost Arbitrage: Cloud brokers dynamically schedule batch jobs on whichever provider currently offers the cheapest compute rate.

Open Cloud Consortium (OCC) (Asked in 2024–25)

The Open Cloud Consortium (OCC) is a non-profit consortium of universities and enterprises that develops open interoperability standards, performance benchmarks, and reference testbeds (such as the Open Science Data Cloud) so heterogeneous clouds can exchange huge datasets smoothly without vendor lock-in.

04 / Core Threats & Defenses (Asked in 2024–25 & 2025–26)

Cloud Security Challenges, Firewalls & Insider Threats

Top 5 Cloud Security Challenges

  1. Data Breaches & Data Loss: Theft or accidental deletion of sensitive customer data due to weak encryption or stolen keys.
  2. Misconfiguration (#1 Real-World Cause): Accidentally leaving Amazon S3 buckets, Kubernetes dashboards, or database ports open to the public internet.
  3. Multi-Tenancy & Shared Infrastructure Risks: Sharing physical CPUs/RAM across customers introduces isolation and side-channel risks.
  4. Account Hijacking & Insecure APIs: Attackers exploiting leaked root credentials or unauthenticated REST endpoints.
  5. Insider Threats: Authorized employees, contractors, or rogue cloud admins abusing valid credentials to steal or sabotage data.

Role of Cloud Firewalls (Asked in 2024–25)

Cloud Firewalls (AWS Security Groups, Network ACLs, Azure Firewall, WAF) inspect and filter inbound/outbound traffic based on IP, port, protocol, and application payload. They block unauthorized SSH/database ports, stop DDoS floods, block SQL injection/XSS attacks (via WAF), and enforce micro-segmentation between public web subnets and private database subnets.

Insider Threats & Mitigation Controls (Asked in 2024–25)

  • Risks: Malicious data theft using valid credentials, sabotage (deleting backups/VMs), and negligent misconfigurations.
  • Mitigation: (1) Least Privilege IAM, (2) Zero Trust + Hardware MFA, (3) Separation of Duties (two-person approval for destructive actions), (4) Customer-Managed Encryption Keys (BYOK) so even provider admins cannot read customer files, and (5) Immutable WORM Audit Logs (AWS CloudTrail) with immediate offboarding revocation.

06 / Governance & Hypervisor Defense

Security Governance & Virtual Machine Security

  • Security Governance: Aligns cloud policies, Shared Responsibility matrices, risk assessments, and legal compliance audits with organizational leadership.
  • VM Escape: Critical attack where malware inside a guest VM exploits a hypervisor vulnerability to gain host OS privileges and compromise all neighboring VMs.
  • VM Sprawl: Uncontrolled creation of forgotten, unpatched VMs that expand the attack surface and waste cloud budget.
  • Side-Channel Attacks: A co-located malicious VM monitors shared CPU L1/L2 cache timings to infer another tenant's cryptographic keys.
  • VM Defenses: Patch hypervisors promptly, use hardened CIS golden images, encrypt virtual disks, isolate VMs with virtual firewalls, and enforce automated VM lifecycle cleanup.

07 / Most Tested Unit IV Topic (2023–24, 2024–25 & 2025–26!)

Identity and Access Management (IAM)

Memory Hook — I-A-A-A: Identification (Who are you?) → Authentication (Prove it via MFA/SSO) → Authorization (Check RBAC/Policy) → Auditing (Log every action).

Evaluating IAM Effectiveness (Strengths vs Limitations)

  • Strengths: MFA stops 99%+ of credential-stuffing attacks; Least Privilege minimizes breach blast radius; IAM Roles eliminate hardcoded passwords; centralized SSO instantly revokes offboarded employee access.
  • Limitations: Vulnerable to human misconfiguration (overly permissive wildcard rules like "Action": "*"), permission sprawl in large microservices, and cannot stop software vulnerabilities alone (must be paired with WAF, encryption, and patching).
